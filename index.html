A stolen key still works.
Its thief’s behaviour doesn’t.

The BES layer checks every action that arrives with a valid signature against how the holder of that key really behaves. It learns how the owner works and how his AI agent works — each from their own behaviour, on their own device — and asks for a second confirmation, or stops the action, when a valid key is being driven by someone, or something, else.

Every security layer before this one stops a forged request. None stops a real key in the wrong hands — a copied password, a leaked API token, an agent’s key taken off a disk. BES asks the question a signature can’t answer: are you behaving like yourself? Credential validity never overrides a behaviour violation. It is the third of four layers: verify-before-parse → the LIGHT barrier → BES → the immune system and tarpit; an action proceeds only if every layer agrees.

Blueprint — organ by organ

Every decision kernel is pure (no clock, no network, no files), total (junk in is a refusal or a step-up, never a crash) and mutation-gated: each operator is flipped and the tests must fail. Counts are read from the witness receipt.

Adoption — what it does for your business

Who it is for

Systems with one owner and high-value keys: founder-run platforms, AI research machines, an operations controller, a studio running its own AI agents on its own keys. One person plus one agent is what it is measured for.

What changes on day one

Nothing visible. It watches for 14 days and enforces nothing. Then the first tier switches on: odd-looking actions are admitted but flagged.

What changes for people

If an action is unusual, you are asked to confirm it once. Your answer teaches it. It never reads what you wrote — it keeps the shape of actions: when, what kind, which project, how big.

What changes for your agents

An agent acts only inside an envelope you signed, with a budget the wallet counts. Money, deals and public claims are never standing permissions: each needs your live yes to those exact words.

Roll-out, step by step

  1. Install beside the node that receives your signed actions. Pin your daily key, your agent’s key, and your offline recovery key (keep that one off the machine).
  2. Sign the agent’s envelope: its verbs, its budget, its hours, its expiry.
  3. Observe for 14 days. Every verdict is recorded and nothing is enforced.
  4. Switch on THROTTLE, then STEP_UP, then BLOCK, then ISOLATE — each only after the one before it has run clean.
  5. Keep the recovery key offline. It is the way back from any block, and it is never used for daily work.

White paper

1 · The gap

The estate’s first layers prove a request is genuine: its sender’s key is pinned, its signature verifies, its counter is fresh, its bytes are LIGHT. Every one of those specs put key compromise out of scope. An attacker holding a valid key passes all of them. BES closes that gap with a different question: not “who are you?” but “are you behaving like yourself?”

2 · Gary W. Floyd’s method

From “GEP-Based Security: Behavioral Entropy Signatures for Cybersecurity and DevOps” (2025): every action is evaluated across three entropy domains — network (H_net), behavioural (H_beh) and authorisation (H_auth) — combined into one state in nats, and a decision function built on the Guided Entropy Principle:

S = w_n·H_net + w_b·H_beh + w_a·H_auth
E(t) = σ(k(|S_current − S_baseline| − ε)) · A(t) = action salience · β|∇S(t)| = damping · ΔS = expected shift

Verdicts ALLOW · THROTTLE · STEP-UP · BLOCK · ISOLATE. “Credential validity does not override entropy violation.” Baselines are built online, with no training corpus, over about two weeks. His key finding: attackers fail by being overly regular (scripted) or randomly variant.

From “Bayesian Entropy Similarity (BES): Alignment Salience for Entropy-Regulated AI Systems” (Technical Report, December 2025): A_t = exp(−D_KL(O‖B) − k·ΔS), and the decision matrix — E above threshold×2.5 → BLOCK, ×2.0 → STEP_UP, ×1.5 → THROTTLE; within bounds, A below its minimum → BLOCK, otherwise ALLOW. “Alignment does not override entropy.” And: “Agent alignment ≠ operator alignment. Track both separately.”

3 · Where the papers are silent, and what this build chose
  • The symbols. IP addresses, geography, ports and MFA devices are not observable on one local machine; the nearest local signals stand in (client surface, gaps, bursts; kind, step, project, size, hour, weekday, session time; permission tier, its transitions, privilege elevation and time since).
  • θ = ⅓, so “within bounds” (E ≤ 1.5θ = ½) is exactly |S − S_b| ≤ ε, and the top rung stays reachable.
  • A(t) is clamped at 1. The paper’s simplified formula exceeds 1 when ΔS < 0, against its own Property 3.1; clamping keeps Properties 3.1–3.3 as stated.
  • ΔS per action is the change it makes to |S − S_b|; the session’s ΔS is its running sum (Gary’s own “delta_s_session”).
  • The two tables are combined: the alignment matrix gives the rung; the security table adds rapid escalation (β|∇S| > 1 → STEP_UP) and critical anomaly (→ ISOLATE).
  • Four parts: the unified S and each of the three domains are judged against their own learned bars, worst wins — a script that only changes its timing collapses H_net while S barely moves.
  • Overly regular gets its own row: the rhythm of the last six positive gaps (mean absolute deviation over the mean, the estate’s v1 measure) below the identity’s own learned floor → BLOCK. An identity that is itself clockwork learns a floor of 0.
  • Bars are learned per identity from its own formation and adapt with damping: ε and k from its own deviations, the alignment bar from its own D_KL + kΔS, β from its own |∇S|.
  • EMA α = 0.2 (the paper’s typical value), applied once per completed block of accepted actions.
  • Quarantine: a flagged genuine action is learned only when its owner answers; nothing nobody vouches for is ever learned. A block that moves the baseline too far is held for the owner to confirm (the paper’s proposed stability bound).
4 · The three-part identity (the estate’s extension)

The owner’s human baseline learns the structure of his irregularity and never rejects it. The agent has its own baseline — never a copy of his — where machine regularity is expected but must be the agent’s own regularity. And a signed delegation envelope: the agent outside it is blocked regardless of its baseline; money, deals, public claims and reserved switches are never in a standing envelope. Owner action = credential ∧ his baseline. Agent action = credential ∧ its baseline ∧ inside the envelope. An agent inside its envelope but not behaving like itself is stepped up to the owner.

5 · Safety rules

No self-lockout: observe-only while forming, and an offline recovery key always lifts a block. Fail toward step-up, never a silent block. Baseline drift adapts with damping; big shifts are confirmed by the owner. Behavioural baselines stay on the device, in LIGHT.

6 · How it was measured

Claims B1–B7 and the predicted limit L1 were sealed first. Real streams: the owner’s human turns, the agent’s tool calls, the agent’s nightly loop. Formation = each stream’s first 14 days; tuning read only the period before 7 October 2026; the claims were measured on 7–28 October. The “stolen key” fixtures only re-time or re-order the identity’s own recorded requests — a fixed pace, machine speed, random moments, the wrong step order, the other identity’s timing, and a perfect copy of last week — injected at 20 moments, each judged on its first ten actions.

7 · Limits

One owner and one agent. Mimicry of one’s own exact past is the method’s known limit. The real numbers are this estate’s, from one machine; Gary’s production results (zero intrusions and under 0.1% false positives over 18 months) are his, not repeated here. No defence against physical compromise.

FAQ

Does it read my messages?

No. It keeps the shape of each action — when, what kind, which project, how large, which permission tier — computed on the device. The text is never stored.

What if I change how I work?

Small changes are learned as you go. A big change is held and you confirm it once. If it ever blocks you, the offline recovery key lifts it.

Isn’t my AI agent supposed to be regular?

Yes — so it is measured against its own regularity, not the owner’s. A script driving its key shows the wrong regularity: a fixed pace, the wrong order, the wrong hours.

Can the agent spend money or sign a deal on its own?

No. Those are never in a standing envelope; each needs the owner’s live approval of the exact words, and changing one character voids it.

Do I need a cloud account?

No. It runs on the machine it protects.
